Bitcoin Slides Below $64,000 as Nvidia's CEO Resigns and a December Rate Hike Becomes a Certainty

Bitcoin fell to its lowest level since August, the world's most valuable chipmaker lost the CEO who built it, and the Federal Reserve is now expected to raise interest rates in December. Three stories, one thread: the market is repricing risk — and here is what every money learner should take from it.

Bitcoin fell below $64,000 over the weekend, its lowest level since August, and now sits roughly 50% below the record of $126,080 it set in October 2025. Ethereum fell further, to around $1,740 — more than 60% below its own record — and the crypto Fear & Greed index printed 13, a reading labelled "Extreme Fear." U.S. spot Bitcoin ETFs lost about $1.07 billion in the week through November 13, their second straight week of billion-dollar outflows, pushing the funds' 2026 flows negative for the first time since they launched. MSCI will also remove Strategy, the largest corporate holder of Bitcoin, from its U.S. index on November 30 — which could force passive funds to sell.

Nvidia Loses the CEO Who Built It

On Thursday, November 12, Jensen Huang resigned as chief executive of Nvidia, ending a 33-year run at the helm of the company he co-founded — and of the AI boom itself. He stepped down after reports of two extramarital affairs, including one with a woman U.S. intelligence officials suspect of being a Chinese intelligence asset. Chief financial officer Colette Kress will serve as interim CEO while the board searches for a permanent successor. Nvidia's shares fell as much as 13% after the announcement, wiping roughly $450 billion off the company's value since the weekend. As the engine of the AI trade, the uncertainty rippled into the wider market — and into Bitcoin, which has tracked tech stocks through this selloff.

The Fed Is Now Expected to Raise Rates in December

The Federal Reserve held its target range at 3.75%–4.00% on October 28, but two data releases since then changed the picture. October's jobs report showed employers added 165,000 jobs — nearly three times what economists expected — with unemployment dipping to 4.1%. Then October's inflation report, released November 10, showed prices up 0.4% in the month, with core inflation up 0.3% for a third straight month. Markets now price a December hike as a near-certainty: the CME FedWatch tool put the odds at 100% as of November 12, up from about 67% a week earlier, and Fed Governor Christopher Waller has floated a larger half-point move. Higher rates make borrowing more expensive, and they tend to pull money away from riskier assets like stocks and crypto — exactly what this week's market action showed.

Scam Alert: AI Is Now Impersonating CEOs

One more story matters for everyday people. U.S. prosecutors have charged three people over an alleged scheme that used AI-generated voices and images to impersonate corporate executives and lawyers, stealing roughly $129 million from companies between 2024 and 2026. If a "CEO" calls asking for an urgent transfer, verify through a channel you trust — a voice or face on a screen is no longer proof of who someone is.

Key Takeaways

  • Flows matter as much as prices: Bitcoin's slide deepened when money flowing into Bitcoin ETFs flipped to outflows. Where the money moves tells you more than the price alone.
  • One company can move the whole market: When the CEO of the world's most valuable company resigns, it shakes stocks, chips, and crypto at once. The AI trade has carried markets for two years.
  • Interest rates connect everything: One jobs report and one inflation report moved rate-hike odds from 67% to 100% in a week, and stocks and crypto felt it immediately. How rates, jobs, and inflation link together turns confusing market news into real money knowledge.
